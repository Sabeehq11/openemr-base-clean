## INR target range for warfarin
For most indications, including atrial fibrillation and venous thromboembolism, warfarin is dosed to an international normalized ratio (INR) of 2.0 to 3.0. Some mechanical heart valves call for a higher target range set by the treating team.

## INR monitoring frequency
INR is checked often after starting warfarin or after any dose change, and then at least every 4 weeks in people whose results are stable. For people with consistently stable INRs, an interval of up to 12 weeks can be reasonable. A missing INR for longer than the planned interval is a monitoring gap.

## Interacting drugs and diet
Antibiotics, antifungals, amiodarone and many other drugs change warfarin effect. INR is checked within about a week of starting or stopping an interacting drug. Large changes in intake of foods rich in vitamin K also change INR.

## High INR without bleeding
When INR is above the therapeutic range but below 10 and there is no bleeding, lowering or holding the next dose is usual. Routine vitamin K is not advised in this case. Bleeding or an INR of 10 or higher needs prompt clinical assessment.
