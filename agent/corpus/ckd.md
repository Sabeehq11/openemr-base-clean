## Definition of chronic kidney disease
Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is present when kidney damage or a reduced eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 persists for more than three months. Kidney damage includes persistent albuminuria. A single abnormal creatinine or eGFR is confirmed with a repeat test before the label is applied.

## Staging by eGFR and albuminuria
CKD is staged by eGFR category, G1 (90 or above) through G5 (below 15), and by albuminuria category, A1 (normal to mildly increased) through A3 (severely increased). Both are reported because risk of progression and of cardiovascular events rises with lower eGFR and higher albuminuria.

## Monitoring frequency
How often kidney function and albuminuria are checked depends on the combined stage. People at higher risk are checked more often, for example at least twice a year at G3 or with moderately increased albuminuria. A rising creatinine or a falling eGFR between tests is reviewed promptly.

## RAS blockade in CKD with albuminuria
An ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor blocker is recommended for people with CKD, hypertension and albuminuria. Serum creatinine and potassium are checked within a few weeks of starting or increasing the dose. A creatinine rise of up to about 30 percent after starting is expected and is not by itself a reason to stop; a larger rise or high potassium is reviewed.
