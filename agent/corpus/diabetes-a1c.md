## Glycemic goals and A1c
A hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) goal below 7 percent is reasonable for many nonpregnant adults with diabetes. A less strict goal, such as below 8 percent, may suit people with limited life expectancy, advanced complications, or a high risk of hypoglycemia. The goal is set with the patient and reviewed over time.

## A1c testing frequency
HbA1c is measured at least twice a year in people whose glycemic control is stable and at goal. It is measured about every three months when therapy has changed or the person is not meeting the goal. A result older than six months in a person with diabetes is a gap in monitoring.

## Kidney screening in diabetes
People with diabetes are screened for kidney disease at least once a year with a urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) and an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) from serum creatinine. Persistent albuminuria or a reduced eGFR points to diabetic kidney disease and changes how medicines are chosen and monitored.

## Metformin and kidney function
Metformin use depends on eGFR. It is not recommended when eGFR is below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2. Starting it is not recommended when eGFR is 30 to 45, and the benefit and risk are reviewed if eGFR falls below 45 during treatment. Kidney function is checked at least yearly in people taking metformin.
